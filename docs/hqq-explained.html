HQQ4 — Half-Quadratic 4-bit Quantization

How we shrank Gemma 3 12B from ~26 GB BF16 to ~9 GB runtime without changing a single downstream tensor shape.

What: post-load weight-only 4-bit quantization Where: src/pipeline.py → _apply_hqq_post_load_quant Flag: GEMMA_POST_LOAD_QUANT=hqq4

1. The problem

Gemma 3 12B holds ~13 billion parameters. Stored as BF16 (2 bytes per parameter) it takes ~26 GB of VRAM. Add the 22 B LTX DiT (~22 GB at FP8), the VAE, the spatial upscaler, and per-step activations, and you need ≥56 GB VRAM — which only H100 80 GB / A100 80 GB have. On 48 GB L40S / RTX 6000 Ada, it doesn't fit.

Quantization is the standard trick: store weights in fewer bits. The naive approach loses accuracy; the sophisticated approaches (GPTQ, AWQ) need calibration data. HQQ is a middle path — no calibration, fast, near-lossless, and the output is a drop-in for any nn.Linear.

2. What "4-bit weight quantization" actually means

A BF16 weight holds 216 ≈ 65 536 distinct values. A 4-bit quantized weight holds only 24 = 16 distinct values. To make this work, we pack consecutive weights into groups and give each group its own scale + zero-point so the 16 values can be rescaled into the range that group actually uses.

The effective per-weight cost for HQQ4 with group size 64 is:

4 bits (index) + 32 bits (scale + zero) ÷ 64 (group size)  =  4 + 0.5  =  4.5 bits/value
vs BF16's 16 bits/value  →  ~3.6× compression

3. The HQQ trick — what makes it different

The naive 4-bit quantizer just rounds each weight to the nearest of 16 levels. That works but leaves accuracy on the table because it ignores that outlier weights in a group force the scale to stretch, pushing the typical values onto just a handful of levels.

Half-Quadratic Quantization (HQQ, Badri & Shaji, 2023) reformulates quantization as an optimization problem that explicitly handles outliers. The goal: 

minimize   ||W - dequant(quant(W, scale, zero))||pp
over       scale, zero  (per group)

Using p < 1 (the authors recommend p = 0.7) makes the loss robust to outliers — a couple of extreme values don't dominate the scale choice. The non-convex optimization is solved with a half-quadratic splitting: introduce an auxiliary variable z, alternate two closed-form sub-problems until convergence.

The critical property: HQQ needs no calibration data. GPTQ and AWQ require a representative batch of tokens to estimate weight sensitivity; HQQ just minimizes the reconstruction loss on the weights themselves. This makes it much faster to apply (seconds per layer, minutes total) and avoids the "wrong calibration set" failure mode.

5. How it plugs into LTX-2

LTX-2 has a twist: the prompt encoder holds lazy builders, not an instantiated Gemma model. The SingleGPUModelBuilder creates Gemma fresh on every PromptEncoder.__call__, uses it inside a gpu_model() context manager, and the context manager's __exit__ moves the model to the meta device to reclaim memory.

Our integration has to (a) materialize Gemma once at init, (b) quantize it, (c) make future builds reuse the cached instance, and (d) prevent gpu_model from emptying our weights between jobs.

7. Pros and cons

ProsCons
Calibration-free. No dataset, no representative prompts. Just runs on the weights themselves. Not activation-aware. AWQ and SmoothQuant use activation statistics to protect sensitive channels. HQQ doesn't — can lose a bit of accuracy on models with heavy activation outliers.
Fast to apply. ~11 s for all 499 Linears in Gemma-12B on Blackwell. GPTQ takes minutes per layer. Boot-time init cost. First cold start on a pod pays ~40 s extra (materialize + quantize).
BF16 activations preserved. Drop-in replacement for nn.Linear; downstream sees the same tensors. Dequant-on-matmul overhead. Each forward pass reconstructs BF16 weights just-in-time. ~3–5 % per-step cost vs native BF16 matmul.
No model fork required. Integrated via 60 lines of post-load monkey-patch; LTX-2 source is untouched. Streaming-incompatible. The packed INT4 + scales + zeros can't be shuffled through LTX-2's per-layer _streaming_model path. HQQ is disabled on <40 GB GPUs.
~3.6× weight compression. 26 GB → 9 GB for Gemma-3-12B — enough to fit on any 48 GB Ada / Blackwell. Kernel variety is thinner than bitsandbytes. On niche archs (Ampere AGX, older consumer cards) bnb might be faster. On H100 / Blackwell / L40S, HQQ is fine.
Near-lossless at 4-bit for Gemma. Observed output-MP4 size (2.4–3.8 MB) matches BF16 baseline output complexity. Not always faster than BF16. On big enough GPUs where VRAM isn't the constraint (H100 80 GB), BF16 can be a hair faster. HQQ wins on cards where streaming would have been needed otherwise.

8. Why we picked HQQ over the alternatives

MethodInterface preserved?Needs calibration?Why we didn't (yet)
W4A16 GPTQ (on-disk checkpoint) Yes Yes LTX-2's custom safetensors loader doesn't understand compressed-tensors format. Produced flat-black output. See gemma-compat-checklist.md.
BitsAndBytes NF4 Yes (compute_dtype=bfloat16) No Equally viable backup. Slightly less performant than HQQ on Blackwell. Kept on the checklist for fallback.
TorchAO Int4WeightOnlyConfig Yes No Raw INT4 without calibration is less accurate than HQQ. Worth trying if we ever need even smaller footprint.
FP8 dynamic (RedHatAI) Yes No Same on-disk loader problem as W4A16. Would also produce black output.
HQQ4 (post-load) Yes No Sidesteps the loader problem, calibration-free, ~17 GB saved. Shipped.
